Design system · couples question app

Depth is rendered as light, not as danger.

The cards get opened at night, on a phone, by two tired people. So the ground is dark and the palette moves from cold morning light to candlelight as the questions get harder. Nothing turns red. Nothing warns you. It just gets later in the evening.

Depth ramp

D1 → D5
Why not a heat map. Green to red is the reflex, and it would be wrong. D5 holds a question about the worst year of someone's childhood. That is not a hazard, it is the most trusting thing in the deck. Colouring it like a fire alarm tells the couple to be afraid of their own conversation.

Domains

Eleven
Held at one luminance. Every domain sits at roughly the same lightness and saturation, so none looks heavier than another. No green for Money, no red for Sex. A colour that editorialises about the subject tells the couple which conversations are the dangerous ones before they have had any of them.

Stakes

27 of 850
Stakes. Both of you unlock this one.

Brass, one weight of hairline, no fill. It is the only colour in the system that appears nowhere else, which is what makes it register. Twenty seven cards carry it out of 850, so it stays rare enough to mean something.

Not red. Red says you have done something wrong. These questions are the ones worth asking most carefully, not the ones to avoid. Brass reads as a key, and a key is an invitation with a condition attached.

Warm paper rather than white. Same ink, same hues at higher contrast. Dark is the default because the deck lives in the evening, but a Saturday morning conversation should not feel like using the app wrong.

Button hierarchy

Four levels

One primary per screen. If a screen appears to need two, the screen is doing two jobs.

Primary · the one action
Secondary · the reasonable alternative
Tertiary · low weight, always available
Unavailable · says why, never just dims
Primary is warm white, not a hue. On a dark ground the highest contrast object available is a near white fill, so the action wins attention without competing with the depth ramp. It also means the button never accidentally implies a mood the question does not have.

Reserved colours

Do not reuse
Brass · stakes unlock only

Brass appears on 27 cards out of 850 and on this one control. Use it on a settings toggle and it stops meaning anything.

Ember · destructive only

Outline, never filled. A filled red button gets pressed by accident.

The one real collision. Ember #CF5A52 sits close to D5 Unspoken #A6604B. They must never appear on the same screen. Destructive actions live in settings and account. D5 lives on cards. Enforce that in code review, not in the palette.

Fields and focus

Input
Rest, then focused
Keyboard focus ring
Focus blue is its own colour. It is the only cool accent in the system and belongs to neither content nor action, which is exactly why it reads as the machine talking rather than the deck.

What has no colour

Deliberate
There is no success green. A finished session gets a sentence, not a tick. Green rewards completion, completion becomes the goal, and a couple starts racing the deck instead of talking. Say That was eleven minutes on Money and stop.
There is no streak colour, no progress bar, no badge. Every one of those turns an intimate conversation into a performance for the app. If you want a metric, keep it on your side of the screen.
There is no warning amber. The only thing worth gating is Stakes, and that already has brass.
