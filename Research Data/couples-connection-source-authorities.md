# Source Authorities for a Couples Connection App

Consolidated reference. Twenty names, grouped by the function they serve in a product build rather than by fame.

---

## Band 1. Clinical and relational authority

The frameworks. Deepest credibility, slowest to translate into software.

### 1. John and Julie Gottman
Books: *The Seven Principles for Making Marriage Work*, *Eight Dates*, *The Love Prescription*
Assets: The Gottman Institute, card deck apps, Gottman Connect, professional training pipeline
Use: Four decades of observational data. The only body of work in the category with genuine predictive validity. Their *Eight Dates* structure is already a sequenced conversation product.
Caution: Competitor first, source second. They ship software.

### 2. Esther Perel
Books: *Mating in Captivity*, *The State of Affairs*
Assets: Podcast *Where Should We Begin?*, Substack *Entre Nous*, the *Where Should We Begin?* card game and its workplace edition, live events, MasterClass
Use: Best living proof that relational IP monetises outside the consulting room. Her territory is desire inside commitment, which is precisely where couples apps go quiet.

### 3. Terry Real
Books: *Us*, *The New Rules of Marriage*, *I Don't Want to Talk About It*
Assets: Relational Life Therapy trainings, heavy podcast presence
Use: Moves people out of "who is right" into a shared relational stance. Useful for reframing screens before prompts.

### 4. Sue Johnson (legacy)
Books: *Hold Me Tight*, *Love Sense*, *Created for Connection*
Note: Died April 2024. Her work continues through ICEEFT.
Use: Emotionally Focused Therapy is the most empirically validated couples model available. *Hold Me Tight* is structured as seven conversations, which is close to a ready made module architecture.

### 5. Alexandra Solomon
Books: *Loving Bravely*, *Love Every Day*, *Taking Sexy Back*
Assets: Podcast *Reimagining Love*
Use: *Love Every Day* is 365 daily prompts. That is the closest published object to a daily app loop.

### 6. Julie Menanno
Book: *Secure Love*
Assets: Instagram @thesecurerelationship, podcast
Use: The best template for compressing attachment theory into short digital units. Study her content structure as much as her content.

### 7. Vienna Pharaon
Book: *The Origins of You*
Assets: Instagram @mindfulmft
Use: Family of origin patterns. Gives depth beyond communication technique.

### 8. Jillian Turecki
Book: *It Begins with You*
Assets: Podcast *Jillian on Love*
Use: Mass audience, self responsibility framing. Good model for a tone that pushes without shaming.

## Band 2. Sex and intimacy

Non negotiable for a couples product. Most connection apps avoid this and lose the users who needed them most.

### 9. Emily Nagoski
Books: *Come As You Are*, *Come Together: The Science (and Art!) of Creating Lasting Sexual Connections*
Use: The most rigorous popular science in the field. Two concepts you should build into the product directly: responsive versus spontaneous desire, and context as the variable that governs arousal. *Come Together* names three characteristics of couples who sustain sexual connection: friendship, prioritising sex, and accepting what is actually true for their own relationship.
Why she matters commercially: she removes shame by removing the idea of normal. That is the unlock for participation.

### 10. Vanessa and Xander Marin
Book: *Sex Talks: The Five Conversations That Will Transform Your Love Life*
Assets: Large social following, paid digital courses
Use: The book is literally five structured conversations. It is a conversation product wearing a book cover. They are also the only sex educators on this list who have already built and sold digital products to couples at scale.

### 11. Justin Lehmiller
Book: *Tell Me What You Want*
Assets: Kinsey Institute researcher, *Sex and Psychology* blog and podcast
Use: Data on fantasy and desire at survey scale. He gives you the empirical backing to write bolder prompts without guessing.

Bench for later: Lori Brotto on mindfulness based sex therapy, Ian Kerner on the clinical conversation about frequency and mismatch.

## Band 3. Mechanic and product builders

These people solved the delivery problem. Study their objects, not their theories.

### 12. Topaz Adizes
Book: *12 Questions for Love*
Assets: The Skin Deep, the Emmy winning series *{THE AND}*, card games, over two million social followers
Use: The only person on this list who treats question design as engineering. His rules are explicit: avoid binary questions, sequence deliberately, build safety before depth. Over 1200 recorded conversations behind the method.
This is your product specification.

### 13. Koreen Odiney
Asset: We're Not Really Strangers, launched 2018
Use: Three level deck structure of Perception, Connection and Reflection. That is an onboarding ladder. She also ran a dating specific expansion inside Bumble, which is the distribution playbook worth copying.

### 14. Steven Bartlett
Asset: The Diary of a CEO Conversation Cards, multiple editions, questions contributed by podcast guests and experts
Use: He productised a ritual, not a framework. Rituals port into software. Also note the sequence: audience first, product second.

### 15. Logan Ury
Book: *How to Not Die Alone*
Role: Behavioural scientist, Hinge
Use: The only person here who has embedded relationship science inside shipped software at scale. Commercially your most instructive study.

### 16. Arthur Aron
Asset: The 36 questions study
Use: The only connection intervention in this entire list with a real experimental foundation. Academic and free. Most consumer products in the category are dressed up derivatives of it.

## Band 4. Adjacent lenses and necessary counterweights

### 17. James J. Sexton
Books: *If You're in My Office, It's Already Too Late*, *How to Stay in Love*
Assets: Podcast *Not Too Late*, extensive podcast circuit
Use: Sells the autopsy rather than the vitamin. His thesis is that gradual disconnection, not betrayal or money, ends marriages. He has solved the emotional trigger problem that prevention products normally fail at.

### 18. Priya Parker
Book: *The Art of Gathering*
Use: Purpose, threshold, rules. Relevant the moment your app touches shared occasions, anniversaries or rituals rather than isolated prompts.

### 19. Charles Duhigg
Book: *Supercommunicators*
Use: Conversation type matching. Practical, testable, reduces cleanly to features.

### 20. Sherry Turkle
Books: *Alone Together*, *Reclaiming Conversation*
Use: Your strongest critic. Her argument is that the device is the cause of the disconnection you propose to fix with a device. Answer her in one sentence or you have a positioning hole.

---

## Demoted from the earlier list

**Marisa Franco** (*Platonic*) and **Kasley Killam** (*The Art and Science of Connection*) both do excellent work, but it is friendship and social health, not couples. With the brief now confirmed as couples, they belong on the shelf, not in the build. Killam returns if you later pursue an employer or insurer channel, because she supplies the health outcome argument.

## Working notes

**The card people beat the therapists on product.** Gottman, Perel, Bartlett, Odiney and Adizes independently converged on the same object. A question is the smallest unit of connection a person can act on immediately.

**Nobody has solved retention.** Every product listed here is a gift purchase with a fast decay curve. Decks get opened twice. Solve the recurring trigger and the rest is sourcing.

**Sex is the retention lever and the risk.** It is the highest intensity content in a couples app and the reason people keep opening it. It is also the fastest way to lose a user who is not ready. Build a consent gate and a depth ladder before you build the content.

**Intellectual property.** Bartlett's decks, We're Not Really Strangers, and the Gottman card decks are protected commercial products with authored question banks. Build your own corpus. Aron and the academic literature are safe ground. If you want to use a named framework, license it properly.

**Local gap.** Every name here is American, Canadian or European. None has built for a South African user on WhatsApp, in eleven languages, with load shedding. That is either your moat or your reason to reconsider.
